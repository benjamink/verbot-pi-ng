System map what talks to what

Five boards hang off the Pi. Only the gearbox is original 1984 hardware — everything else replaces the controller board that was removed.

The interrogation ribbon 9-core, gearbox → Pi

A drum inside the gearbox turns eight cams past eight normally-open switches. Each closes in a fixed sequence, telling the software which gear set is currently selected. All eight return through the single white ground.

How an action actually runs. Drive the motor anti-clockwise until the switch for the action you want closes, then immediately reverse it. That engages a clutch: the drum stops, and a shaft inside it starts turning the selected gear set. Direction alone chooses between hunting and doing.

Power tree one rail, one known compromise

A single 5V bank feeds everything through the OnOff SHIM's load switch. That includes the motor, which is the part worth thinking about.

Two things that will bite you

Cap the action speed. The motor is a 3 V part sitting on a 5 V rail. Keep VERBOT_ACTION_SPEED near ±60 rather than ±100, or you are overdriving it every time it moves.

Never feed VCC from the Pi's 3V3 pin to get a nominal 3 V. That rail comes off the PMIC and cannot supply a stalled motor. VCC is the motor supply, not a logic rail — whatever you apply is what the motor sees.

Front panel the part that isn't confirmed

The eight original red buttons map one-to-one onto the eight gearbox actions, exactly as the toy did in 1984. They hang off the expander's port A as active-low inputs with pull-ups enabled.

Verify before you wire this

This assumes the eight buttons are independent switches to a common rail. That has never been confirmed on the real panel — they may well be a matrix. Check with a meter first.

The original PCB also carried the power switching for both battery rails, so the cleanest approach is to bypass that board entirely and solder to the switch contacts directly.

The button order above is a guess. If a button triggers the wrong action at bring-up, reorder BUTTON_ORDER rather than rewiring.
